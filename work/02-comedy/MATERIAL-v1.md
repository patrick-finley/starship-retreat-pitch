# Comedy first pass — infrastructure theater and ugly economics

**State: proposals, not a selected company premise or script.** Parker owns the story decision; Nadav owns branding and deck. This pass responds to Patrick's request for sharper space/rural-data-center jokes, an adult truck-stop angle, Forbes/prison material, and a concrete Grok handoff.

## Recommended comic mechanism

Every impressive infrastructure claim collapses under one ordinary question into a credit card, a fee, or a founder perk. The audience recognizes how the respectable vocabulary works and catches itself nodding. Play the founders' conviction straight.

Truck stops supply real-world seediness and petty commerce. Orbit supplies extravagant ambition and convenient assumptions. Airline loyalty supplies the actual money-making machine. The joke gets better when the financial product is somehow sleazier than the adult roadside business.

This is a comedy mechanism for Parker to consider, not a decision to lock the business model. Do not revive the rejected deck's title, taquitos, or art direction.

## Three situations from the prompt

**C01 — ★ Launch economics / spoken**
“You'll notice launch costs are zero. That assumption was independently verified by a second founder using the same Elon tweet.”
Setup: show our fictional cost model, with launch priced at zero. No actual tweet or real cost claim is needed. Don't put invented wording in a real person's mouth.

**C02 — ★ Adult roadside business / spoken**
“We considered a truck-stop brothel. But customers would expect to receive something after paying. The airline model gives us more flexibility.”
Setup: a brief comparison of business models. The joke is the company's willingness to sell benefits it won't deliver. Keep the service reference non-graphic.

**C03 — Product / spoken**
“Our customer drives 120,000 miles a year and has never been upgraded. That is a fucking underserved market.”
Setup: an invented customer persona; the mileage is comedy copy, not researched market evidence.

## Three room-specific lines

**C04 — Rural data-center peers**
“We asked rural data-center founders about their biggest problems: power, cooling, and getting technicians to the site. We heard them. We made it harder to visit.”
Setup: fictional customer-discovery dialogue, not a claim about actual interviews or named companies.

**C05 — ★ Forbes team / Patrick**
“Nadav and Parker are both Forbes 30 Under 30. I'm Patrick. My role in the documentary is to say, ‘Looking back, there were signs.’”
Setup: founders slide. A prediction about this fictional company, not a reference to a real fraud. Team supplied the Forbes background.

**C06 — Will**
“Will is here to explain why this can't work. We listed that as technical engagement.”
Setup: advisor slide. Fictional treatment of our company, not an actual quote or agreed advisory role. Professional background source: https://www.starship.vc/team.

## Three YC credential bits

**C07 — Scope inflation**
“We're YC Summer '26. Our first company was a credit card. Then a partner asked whether we were thinking big enough. That conversation cost four billion dollars.”
Setup: invented company origin story; fake YC affiliation, fake partner conversation, fake budget.

**C08 — Change of customer**
“YC told us to make something people want. So we made the deck say whatever the people with money wanted.”
Setup: the company's interpretation of accelerator advice, not a real conversation.

**C09 — Pivot**
“We pivoted from truck stops to space. Same business. Nobody at Demo Day wanted to tell their parents they'd invested in a truck stop.”
Setup: fictional founder rationale. Best used if that motive fits Parker's chosen premise.

## Three visual/performance bits

**C10 — Imaginary diligence**
Cost model: launch = $0. Clicking the source note reveals “See other founder's spreadsheet.” The other founder's spreadsheet cites the first.
Use a simple two-slide reveal or physically pass the laptop back. Land it quickly; don't explain circular sourcing.

**C11 — Patrick's credential**
Two founder portraits carry Forbes 30 Under 30 credentials. Patrick's equally grand label reads “DOCUMENTARY INTERVIEW — BOOKED.”
Alternate label: “EMERGENCY CONTACT.” Let the asymmetry do the work.

**C12 — Actual workload**
A magnificently overbuilt orbital-compute schematic resolves into one small airline redemption message: “No award availability.”
The machine is working perfectly. The outcome is just useless to the customer.

Roles here are performance suggestions; they do not change workstream ownership.

## What to ask Grok for

Use GROK-PUNCHUP-v1.md. Have it compete on adult roadside-business satire, meaner founder/VC roasts, and short three-person exchanges. Ask it to identify our weakest lines, not just elaborate them. Bring its answer back for selection; output volume is not a measure of quality.

The serial-killer/sex-worker-murder direction is a familiar shock detour. It does little to connect the three assigned elements or expose this room's behavior. The brothel comparison gives us a sharper reversal when the airline economics are worse.
