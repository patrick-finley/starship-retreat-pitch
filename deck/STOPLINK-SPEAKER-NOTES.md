# STOPLINK — stage script

Target: around 3 minutes, plus laughter. First three slides straight. Pause on the orange reveal.

## 1. STOPLINK

PARKER: America runs on the interstate. We are building the infrastructure above it. STOPLINK is orbital compute for the American truck-stop economy. Our orbital GPU clusters deliver AI dispatch, route optimization, and predictive maintenance to fleets at truck stops. We put the data center in orbit and the customer terminal beside the diesel pump. Deliver straight, with no wink.
Source note: YC affiliation is fictional retreat-pitch parody. YC mark from https://www.ycombinator.com/press.

## 2. What the company does

PARKER: We sell airline miles to truck stops. The truck stops buy our miles to reward diesel purchases and keep drivers coming back. Drivers redeem those miles for flights on existing airlines. To win the contract, we bundle free AI from our orbital data centers. PATRICK: People who drive for a living earn enough points to finally stop fucking driving.

## 3. The orbital layer

NADAV: Truck-stop terminals uplink fleet data to GPU data centers in orbit. Those GPUs run routing and maintenance models, then send predictions back to the terminal. That is the actual product we claim to build. Solar-powered orbital compute for the interstate. We went where the power is: straight up. We have taken an ordinary transaction and made it dependent on a rocket launch. Do not claim free power, easy cooling, or proven engineering. This is the fictional architecture.

## 4. The revenue reveal

PARKER: Here is the revenue model. We sell miles. Every compute contract includes an airline loyalty program. Truck stops buy miles from us. Drivers earn them on fuel and fleets earn them on compute. We give away the compute; the miles are the entire revenue model. Airlines already sell miles to commercial partners. We kept that business and deleted the airline. Drivers earn STOPLINK miles at truck stops and use them to book flights on existing airlines. We do not operate aircraft. PATRICK: The first airline with no airplanes. Our fleet has never been late. Our fleet is a font.
Research: United 2025 Form 10-K confirms mileage revenue from non-airline partners and its co-brand agreement. It does NOT establish the claim that United makes more money selling miles than flying planes, or that all loyalty programs are worth more than their airlines. Those unsupported comparisons have been removed. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100517/000010051726000023/ual-20251231.htm

## 5. How the money moves

PARKER: A truck stop signs an orbital compute contract, but its bill is for a recurring purchase of miles. Drivers earn miles on fuel; fleets earn miles using the compute. A co-branded card could extend this to other spending. When the driver redeems, we pay for a flight on an existing airline. We keep the spread after reward costs and operating costs. A co-branded card can add partner revenue later. No bank or airline partnership is signed in this fictional pitch. PATRICK: People who drive for a living earn enough points to finally stop fucking driving. Reward costs are real; we do not assume nobody redeems.

## 6. The signup bonus

NADAV: Why put the data centers in orbit? Solar-powered GPUs are the product story. The free compute gets truck stops to sign the miles contract. PATRICK: The data center is a six-hundred-million-dollar signup bonus. PARKER: Does that make economic sense? PATRICK: Our unit economics exclude everything in space. NADAV: Sean and Hugo call that a seed round. PATRICK: We call it customer acquisition. The six hundred million is a fictional joke estimate.
